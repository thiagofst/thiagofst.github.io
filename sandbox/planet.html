How to use. Pick a whole system under Systems, choose the host (a single star, a white dwarf, or two stars), then edit the planets. Drag the time bar or press play; drag the 3D view to rotate it. Freeze the system as a comparison, change anything, and the grey dashed curves show what it was before. Add up to six planets. Evolution shows tidal circularisation and Kozai–Lidov cycles for the selected planet.

Model. Every planet follows its own Kepler orbit, with no planet–planet interaction. The light curve, the Rossiter–McLaughlin signal and the radial velocity are sums over planets. Orbit and sky coordinates: Winn 2010. Transit: quadratic limb darkening integrated in concentric rings. Rossiter–McLaughlin: first-moment shift of the unblocked light of a rigidly rotating star (Ohta, Taruya & Suto 2005); λ > 0 tilts the projected stellar spin axis toward the planet's direction of motion.

Two stars. P-type (circumbinary): the planets orbit both stars. S-type: the planet orbits one star and the other is a distant companion. The stars move on their Kepler orbit about the barycentre, a P-type planet orbits the barycentre with the total mass of the binary, an S-type planet orbits its own star, and each planet transits whichever star it crosses. The stars eclipse each other as uniform discs, so their mutual eclipses ignore limb darkening, and the secondary shares the primary's limb darkening and has no spin signal. Stability limits are the fits of Holman & Wiegert 1999 for circular, coplanar orbits (P-type as written in Martin, Mazeh & Fabrycky 2015, S-type as in Haghighipour 2010; uncertainty about 5%), with μ the companion's mass fraction. Orbits that would pass inside the star or its Roche limit, 2.44 R★(ρ★/ρp)^⅓, are corrected automatically and the readout says what changed. No ellipsoidal variation, reflection or beaming, so very close binaries are out of range.

Star surface. Spots and plages sit on a rotating pixel map of the stellar disc (single-star hosts). A spot is a blackbody of Teff minus ΔT, a plage of Teff plus ΔT(μ) = 250.9 − 407.7 μ + 190.9 μ² K (×the scale you set), both at 0.8 µm; the default spot ΔT of 663 K, the plage law, the quiet-Sun convective blueshift of 285 m/s (vertical, projected by μ) and its one-third reduction in magnetic regions are the solar values of Meunier, Desort & Lagrange 2010; for other stars they are illustrative, and I apply the same reduction inside spots. The rotation period follows from v sin i, i★ and the radius. The light curve is normalised to the unspotted star, the radial velocity is the planets plus the activity signal (rotation of the flux-weighted disc and blueshift suppression), and a planet crossing a spot shows the bump and the changed Rossiter–McLaughlin shape. Not modelled: differential rotation, spot evolution, penumbrae, and the limb dependence of the blueshift beyond μ.

Radii from mass. Planets and stars: the broken power law of Chen & Kipping 2017, a mean radius rather than a prediction for any single planet (move the radius slider to override). White dwarfs: the Eggleton relation of Verbunt & Rappaport 1988, as written in arXiv:0908.2116, Appendix A. Planet types follow common usage; the period boundaries of the Neptunian desert, ridge and savanna are from Castro-González et al. 2024. Presets are generic, not specific systems.

Tides: Goldreich & Soter 1966 in the form of Matsumura et al. 2008. Kozai–Lidov: Fabrycky & Tremaine 2007, Naoz 2016.
